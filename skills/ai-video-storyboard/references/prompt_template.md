## 0. Select the Requested Deliverable

Use only the prompt type the user requests. The user may invoke the skill and describe the task naturally, or use one of these short mode labels:

```text
MODE: STORYBOARD | FILM POSTER | CHARACTER BIBLE | LOCATION BIBLE | SCREENPLAY / SHOT PLAN | VIDEO PROMPT
Project/title: [exact name]
Source material: [script, image references, or established facts]
Output/prompt language: [e.g. English]
Aspect ratio and duration: [when relevant]
Image roles: [identity/design, location, layout-only, style-only, start/bridge frame]
Requested deliverable: [what to create now]
```

The fields are optional unless the detail is necessary to avoid a material continuity error. If the user requests a single poster or design board, do not attach a storyboard/video workflow. If the user requests a video prompt, follow the storyboard review order unless an approved board is supplied or the user explicitly chooses another order.

### Storyboard timing rule

A scene is a dramatic unit and may exceed 10 seconds. A generation unit is one video file and must stay within the user's/target tool's cap (default maximum: 10 seconds in this workflow). A generation storyboard covers one unit; a longer scene board is an editorial shot plan with timecodes, generation-unit mapping, and matching end/start states. Never label a long scene as one 10-second generation.

### Reference-role labels

State each uploaded image's job in the prompt: `IDENTITY / DESIGN`, `LOCATION`, `LAYOUT ONLY`, `STYLE ONLY`, or `START / BRIDGE FRAME`. A layout/style image must not overwrite character identity. For image-to-video, use a single approved frame as the start image; use a full contact sheet only in a storyboard/reference slot that supports it, not as a start frame.

## 1. The Master Screenplay & Scriptwriting Engine

Use this section to write or adapt any script before moving to visual breakdown.

### A. Cinematic Feature / Short Film Scene Template
```markdown
# SCENE [##]: [LOCATION] - [INT./EXT.] - [TIME OF DAY]

[ATMOSPHERE & SETTING]:
Brief 2-line scene establishing the environmental temperature, lighting, and soundscape.

[CHARACTERS PRESENT]:
- [CHARACTER NAME]: [State of mind, physical condition, costume].

[ACTION BLOCK]:
Present-tense, visceral description of physical actions, spatial movement, and optical beats. 
Describe Newtonian physics (inertia, gravity, foot traction).

[CHARACTER NAME]
(emotional delivery / physical state)
"Dialogue line."

[REACTION & PACING BEAT]:
Visual reaction, eye contact, non-verbal cues.
```

### B. High-End Commercial Advertising Template (The AIDA Framework)
```markdown
# COMMERCIAL SCRIPT: [BRAND / PRODUCT NAME] (TARGET: 15–30 SECONDS)

[0:00–0:03] ATTENTION (THE SENSORY HOOK):
- Visual: Phantom Flex 4K ultra-macro high-speed shot of the product, crisp volumetric lighting, bold dynamic movement.
- Audio: Sharp sensory Foley (crisp snap, effervescent fizz, fluid splash, metallic click) + immediate sonic branding.

[0:03–0:08] INTEREST (KINETIC DEMONSTRATION):
- Visual: Fast-paced dynamic interaction; the hero problem solved in real time with effortless elegance.
- Voiceover / Text: Provocative solution-focused proposition.

[0:08–0:20] DESIRE (ASPIRATIONAL LIFESTYLE PAYOFF):
- Visual: Seamless transition into premium lifestyle context. Character experiencing emotional gratification and elevation.
- Atmosphere: Warm, radiant luxury color grading (Alexa 65, 3200K golden light or crisp clean modern daylight).

[0:20–0:30] ACTION (THE HERO PACKSHOT & CTA):
- Visual: Pristine, locked-down hero packshot with subtle light flare passing across the logo.
- Audio: Resonant brand mnemonic chord + voiceover command.
- Text Slate: Elegant typography logo, website, and bold call-to-action.
```

### C. Viral Short-Form Video (Reels / TikTok / Shorts) Template

```markdown
[0:28–0:30] THE INFINITE LOOP ANCHOR:
- Last action seamlessly flows into the first frame at 0:00.
```

## 2. Aspect-Ratio-Aware Storyboard Sheets

### Formula A: 16:9 Widescreen Storyboard Sheet (4×3 Grid — 12 Panels)
```text
A professional 16:9 widescreen cinematic film production contact sheet formatted in a 4-COLUMN by 3-ROW GRID (12 panels total: 10 sequential 16:9 widescreen live-action movie panels + 2 technical slate cards).

PROJECT-SPECIFIC CONTINUITY AND FORMAT:
1. Every individual image panel inside the grid is strictly a wide horizontal 16:9 rectangular cinematic frame, NOT square, NOT vertical.
2. Maintain character, costume, prop, and location consistency with the supplied references and screenplay.
3. If a previous-segment bridge frame exists, Panel 1 matches it; for a first scene without a bridge, Panel 1 follows the approved opening composition instead.
4. Lock the action axis and screen positions only where continuity requires it; state any motivated crossing or reversal.
5. Include weather, particles, fog, or volumetric light only when established by the script or approved look references.
6. Match the requested visual medium. Use photorealistic live action only when realism is requested; do not add a camera brand, lens format, or CGI prohibition as a substitute for a clear visual brief.
7. Top header: 'SCENE TITLE: "[TITLE]" | SEGMENT [##] ([START_TIME] - [END_TIME]) | PAGE: [P] OF [N]'.

ROW 1 (PANELS 1 TO 4):
- Panel 1: [EXACT BRIDGE FRAME if continuing; otherwise approved opening frame].
- Panel 2: [NEXT TIME-CODED ACTION / CAMERA STATE].
- Panel 3: [NEXT SCRIPTED SHOT OR DETAIL].
- Panel 4: [NEXT SCRIPTED SHOT OR REACTION].

ROW 2 (PANELS 5 TO 8):
- Panel 5: [NEXT TIME-CODED SHOT].
- Panel 6: [CHARACTER REACTION OR ACTION RESULT].
- Panel 7: [NEXT CAMERA SETUP / MOVEMENT].
- Panel 8: [DETAIL INSERT only if the script calls for it].

ROW 3 (PANELS 9 TO 12):
- Panel 9: [PENULTIMATE TIME-CODED SHOT / RESULT].
- Panel 10: [UNIT END FRAME AND CONTINUITY STATE].
- Panel 11: Dark grey film production slate card titled 'AUDIO DNA PROFILE' with frequency waveforms and sound mix notes.
- Panel 12: Compact 'DIRECTOR LOGIC AUDIT' with checks for source fidelity, identity, timing, camera continuity, and generation cap. Do not pre-mark a board as approved before inspection.
```

## 3. Conditional Visual-to-Kinematic Spatial Geometry Audit

When the shot includes contact, a prop handoff, or spatially precise interaction and a relevant frame/reference exists, run this 5-point audit. Skip it for unrelated deliverables:
1. **Pose & Posture Geometry**: Exact physical posture of characters (standing, kneeling, crouching, prone).
2. **Hand Coordinates & Prop Anchor**: 2D/3D location of each hand and what it holds.
3. **Spatial Reachability Gate**:
   - Reach $\le 15\%$ frame width: Direct planar touch allowed.
   - Reach $> 15\%$ frame width: Must command full-body displacement or cut to medium coverage before contact.
4. **Target Anatomical Localization**: Naming exact anatomical landing coordinates (e.g. "front-left shoulder blade").
5. **Spatial Negative Exclusion**: Barring neighboring anatomy from receiving props.

## 5. Anti-Hallucination & Zero-Spin Engine (The Dual-Mode Director)

### Mode A: The Continuous Plan-Séquence / Steadicam Unidirectional Take
Used when maintaining a single, unbroken camera take from a bridge frame anchor:
```text
CAMERA PERSPECTIVE & UNIDIRECTIONAL FORWARD TRACKING (STEADICAM FOLLOW-SHOT):
The camera is a steady, low-angle Steadicam follow-shot positioned directly BEHIND (or in FRONT OF) both characters, tracking continuously forward through the terrain at their exact walking pace from Second 0:00 to Second 0:10.
Both characters walk continuously forward AWAY FROM (or TOWARD) the camera into the scene toward the destination ahead.

CRITICAL ANTI-SPIN & ZERO-ROTATION MANDATE (NO TURNING AROUND):
1. Both characters WALK IN ONE CONTINUOUS UNIDIRECTIONAL FORWARD PATH throughout all 10 seconds!
2. ABSOLUTELY DO NOT TURN AROUND TO FACE THE CAMERA!
3. ABSOLUTELY DO NOT ROTATE THE CHARACTERS 180 DEGREES!
4. ZERO TURNTABLE SPINNING, ZERO CAMERA ORBIT, ZERO REVERSE-ANGLE FLIPS!
5. Maintain unbroken linear motion throughout the entire shot.
```

### Mode B: Hollywood Cinematic Coverage & Cut Syntax
Used when commanding the video model to execute multi-panel storyboard edits across one generation unit (illustrative example — replace with the user's scripted beats):
```text
[0:00–0:02: MASTER WIDE (Panel 1)]
[Approved characters] move forward into [approved environment] (bridge/start state).
[0:02–0:04: CUT TO: LOW-ANGLE INSERT (Panel 3)]
Low-angle tracking on [approved detail: footwear / prop / terrain contact].
[0:04–0:06: CUT TO: MEDIUM TWO-SHOT (Panel 5)]
[Approved spatial relationship between characters per script].
[0:06–0:08: CUT TO: MACRO/DETAIL INSERT (Panel 8)]
Macro close-up: [one scripted tactile detail only if the scene contains it].
[0:08–0:10: DISSOLVE TO: WIDE REVEAL (Panel 10)]
[Destination/result reveal per script; exact unit end state].
```

## 7. Universal Hollywood Pre-Flight Safety-Shield

This is an optional wording aid, not a guarantee that a platform will accept a prompt. Use it only when appropriate to the user's scene; do not automatically change the genre, stakes, or events.

### Optional Fictional-Production Framing:
```text
[OPTIONAL PRODUCTION CONTEXT]:
A scripted scene from [PROJECT / GENRE], staged as a fictional cinematic production. Preserve the user's intended tone, action, and stakes. [Describe practical/stunt context only when relevant.]
```

### Optional illustrative safety-language examples (use only when they fit the story; never rewrite plot/tone to force them):
- `violent storm / gale` $\rightarrow$ `brisk gust of wind / dynamic atmospheric flurries`
- `brutal cold / screaming wind` $\rightarrow$ `challenging terrain / brisk breeze`
- `blood / gory wound` $\rightarrow$ `theatrical stunt prosthetic makeup (only if the script calls for staged practice)`
- `bandage / medical dressing` $\rightarrow$ `protective thermal patch / fabric tag (only if scripted)`
- `stumbles heavily / collapses in pain` $\rightarrow$ `lowers center of gravity to steady balance`
- `exhausted / freezing` $\rightarrow$ `showing focused resolve and endurance`
- `wild predator` $\rightarrow$ `calm, cooperative trained studio animal (only if the production uses one)`

## 8. Action Beat Timing (Use When Helpful)

For a generation unit up to 10 seconds, assign timecodes to the actual action and camera beats. A useful generic scaffold is: opening state; action initiation; interaction or reaction; consequence; exact end state. Split more finely when the action requires it. For quiet or macro scenes, use the real changes in framing, light, focus, sound, or stillness; do not invent physical action to fill the timeline.

## 9. The Sheet-Driven Storyboard-to-Video Master Formula

When uploading `storyboard_sheet.jpg` to Google Veo / Gemini Video / Kling / Runway:

If the video tool treats an uploaded image as the literal starting frame, use the approved single start/bridge frame—not the full storyboard contact sheet. Upload the whole sheet only as a separate visual-reference input when the tool supports that role. The zero-grid instruction applies to the rendered video; it does not ban grids on the storyboard itself.
```text
[CRITICAL ZERO-GRID MANDATE — INSTANT FULL-SCREEN LIVE ACTION AT 0:00S]:
From the very first frame at 0.00 seconds, the video MUST BE 100% FULL-SCREEN LIVE ACTION CINEMA.
STRICT NEGATIVE: ABSOLUTELY DO NOT SHOW THE STORYBOARD SHEET, DO NOT SHOW THE 12-PANEL GRID, DO NOT SHOW WHITE OR BLACK FRAMES/BORDERS, AND DO NOT DISPLAY TEXT CAPTIONS IN THE FIRST SECOND! The video MUST NOT start on the contact sheet; it starts 100% zoomed in and rendered as pure full-screen live action inside the real scene from Frame 0.

[SAFETY & PRODUCTION FRAMEWORK]:
[OPTIONAL: use staged-production language only if it fits the story and target platform. Do not change the requested genre, reduce the intended stakes, or claim guaranteed platform acceptance.]
Render as [requested medium/style] in [aspect ratio], with authentic textures and physically coherent movement appropriate to the approved references.

DIRECT SHEET-DRIVEN EXECUTION (PANELS 1 TO 10):
Translate the approved storyboard for [GENERATION UNIT AND TIMECODE] into full-screen video. Follow the actual panel order, scripted action, and camera instructions; do not invent beats to fill time. Use the specified shot/cut mode and end on the recorded continuity state:

- 0:00–0:02 — [Opening image, first action/camera state, or deliberate stillness].
- 0:02–0:04 — [Next visible development from the approved board].
- 0:04–0:06 — [Action/reaction and motivated camera change, if any].
- 0:06–0:08 — [Consequence or continued movement].
- 0:08–0:10 — [Final action and exact end state; stop at the unit boundary].

CHARACTER, PROP & AXIS CONTINUITY (AS APPLICABLE):
- Identity/appearance: [approved reference and invariants].
- Screen positions/action axis: [only if relevant; preserve or state a motivated change].
- Props and physical state: [ownership, position, and end/start state].

ENVIRONMENT AND ATMOSPHERE:
[Scripted weather, particles, fog, set dressing, or clean atmosphere; maintain continuity without adding unrequested effects.]

CAMERA, LIGHT & SOUND:
[Shot size/lens if useful, motivated movement, lighting/color, dialogue, effects, music, and transition. Use only details established for this unit.]
```

## 12. Real-World Production Failure Modes & Architectural Case Studies

| Case | Visual Defect | Root Cause in AI Models | v7.0 Hollywood Solution |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Case 1** | Prop spawns out of clothing fabric. | Lack of mechanical causality and negative lexicon trap. | **4-Step Mechanical Chain** (hand to fastener $\rightarrow$ open $\rightarrow$ reach in $\rightarrow$ extract). |
| **Case 2** | Character spins 180° / prop mark jumps sides. | 3D rotational verbs (`wraps around`) force back view. | **Planar Surface Interactions** (`places flat directly over`) + 180° camera lock. |
| **Case 3** | Prop lands on wrong anatomy. | Reach distance in starting frame too large. | **Reachability Gate Audit** (cut to medium action before contact) + anatomical exclusions. |
| **Case 4** | Contact sheet appears on screen in seconds 0–3. | Video model treats sheet as static image to animate. | **`CRITICAL ZERO-GRID MANDATE`** with strict negative against borders, grid, and captions. |
| **Case 5** | Characters freeze; atmosphere vanishes. | Model prior inertia and atmospheric smoothing. | **Explicit per-beat motion + atmosphere persistence**. |
| **Case 6** | Characters spin like a turntable mid-shot. | Conflict between rear start frame and frontal descriptions. | **Mode A (Steadicam Unidirectional Take)** or **Mode B (Hollywood Coverage Cuts)**. |
| **Case 7** | Adult face de-ages into a child face. | Diffusion smoothing in wide shots. | **Adult Skeletal Identity Lock** (approved age/bone structure) + negative child tokens. |
| **Case 8** | System refusal ("I can't generate that video"). | Words triggering platform filters. | **Safety-aware rewording**; follow platform rules, no bypass claims. |
| **Case 9** | Video becomes a monotonous movement loop. | Lack of physical sub-actions across the unit. | **Biomechanical Micro-Action Staging** (scripted milestone progression). |
